Technical, Security, and Compliance Go-Live Test Plan
1. Purpose
This plan defines the technical, security, resilience, privacy, and compliance activities required before the application is approved for production launch.
The objectives are to:
•	Confirm that the application and APIs function correctly under normal and adverse conditions.
•	Identify and remediate security vulnerabilities.
•	Verify that actual data-processing practices match published policies and declarations.
•	Validate backup, recovery, incident response, and cloud-resilience procedures.
•	Document residual risks and obtain formal approval from accountable decision-makers.
2. Scope
The assessment should cover:
•	Android mobile application.
•	Public and internal APIs.
•	Authentication and authorization services.
•	Administrative portals and back-office systems.
•	Databases, storage, queues, and supporting infrastructure.
•	Production cloud accounts, networks, firewalls, and access controls.
•	Third-party SDKs, analytics tools, payment providers, and external services.
•	User account, consent, deletion, refund, reporting, and appeal workflows.
•	Backup, disaster-recovery, logging, monitoring, and incident-response processes.
3. Proposed Schedule
The plan can normally be completed over five to six weeks, depending on application complexity and the number of findings.
Phase	Activities	Indicative Duration
1	Planning, scope confirmation, and test readiness	3–5 business days
2	Functional, performance, compatibility, and resilience testing	1–2 weeks
3	Security testing, scanning, and penetration testing	1–2 weeks
4	Privacy and compliance audit	1 week
5	Disaster-recovery and incident-response exercises	2–4 business days
6	Remediation, retesting, risk acceptance, and go-live decision	1–2 weeks
Testing activities may run in parallel where environments and resources allow.
4. Roles and Responsibilities
Role	Main Responsibilities
Product Owner	Confirms business requirements, pricing, refunds, user journeys, and launch priorities
QA Lead	Owns functional, compatibility, performance, and regression testing
Security Lead	Owns security testing, penetration testing, vulnerability triage, and security sign-off
Engineering Lead	Coordinates technical fixes and confirms deployment readiness
DevOps/Cloud Lead	Reviews production configuration, network security, backups, recovery, and monitoring
Privacy/Compliance Lead	Verifies privacy notices, consent, data flows, deletion, retention, and third-party transfers
Legal Counsel	Reviews regulatory obligations, lawful retention, evidence handling, and formal exceptions
Customer Support/Operations	Validates complaints, refunds, reporting, escalation, and incident procedures
Executive Risk Owner	Accepts residual risks and authorizes or rejects production launch
5. Phase 1: Planning and Test Readiness
Activities
1.	Confirm the systems, applications, APIs, environments, and third parties included in scope.
2.	Prepare an architecture diagram showing:
o	Application components.
o	API connections.
o	Databases and storage.
o	Authentication services.
o	Third-party SDKs.
o	Data flows and transfer locations.
3.	Prepare a data inventory and data-flow map covering:
o	Personal and sensitive data collected.
o	Purpose of collection.
o	Storage location.
o	Retention period.
o	Third-party recipients.
o	Transfers outside Egypt.
4.	Confirm that a production-like test environment is available.
5.	Prepare test accounts for different roles and permission levels.
6.	Define severity classifications, escalation paths, and remediation deadlines.
7.	Confirm access to:
o	Source code.
o	API documentation.
o	Cloud configuration.
o	Application builds.
o	Logging and monitoring systems.
o	Privacy policies and store declarations.
o	Backup and recovery procedures.
Entry Criteria
Testing may begin when:
•	The release candidate is stable and version-controlled.
•	Test environments and accounts are available.
•	Architecture and data-flow documentation has been provided.
•	Known limitations are documented.
•	Security testers have written authorization to test the systems.
6. Phase 2: Technical QA and Resilience Testing
6.1 Functional Testing
Validate all critical user journeys, including:
•	Account creation and verification.
•	Login, logout, password reset, and OTP verification.
•	Profile and account management.
•	Core application functions.
•	Payments, subscriptions, cancellations, and refunds, where applicable.
•	Notifications and communications.
•	User reporting, review, and appeal processes, where applicable.
•	Account deletion through the application and web link.
•	Customer-support and escalation paths.
For each test, record:
•	Test case ID.
•	Preconditions.
•	Steps performed.
•	Expected result.
•	Actual result.
•	Evidence.
•	Pass/fail status.
•	Related defect ID.
6.2 Performance and Load Testing
Test:
•	Expected normal traffic.
•	Forecast peak traffic.
•	Sudden traffic spikes.
•	Sustained load.
•	High API concurrency.
•	Database-intensive operations.
•	OTP and authentication service limits.
•	Rate limiting and abuse controls.
•	Recovery after overload.
Record:
•	Response times.
•	Error rates.
•	Throughput.
•	Resource utilization.
•	Queue depth.
•	Database performance.
•	Time required for the service to recover.
Performance thresholds should be approved before testing begins.
6.3 Network Interruption Testing
Test application behavior during:
•	Complete loss of connectivity.
•	Slow or unstable connections.
•	Switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data.
•	Interrupted uploads or transactions.
•	Duplicate requests caused by retries.
•	Delayed API responses.
•	Application termination during a transaction.
Confirm that the application:
•	Does not corrupt or duplicate data.
•	Provides understandable error messages.
•	Safely retries only appropriate operations.
•	Does not expose sensitive information.
•	Returns to a consistent state after connectivity is restored.
6.4 Android Compatibility Testing
Test representative combinations of:
•	Supported Android versions.
•	Different manufacturers.
•	Low-, medium-, and high-specification devices.
•	Different screen sizes and resolutions.
•	Limited memory and storage.
•	Battery-saving modes.
•	Accessibility settings.
•	Arabic and English layouts, including right-to-left display where applicable.
•	Upgrade from the previous application version.
•	Clean installation and reinstall scenarios.
6.5 Technical QA Exit Criteria
•	All planned critical test cases have been executed.
•	All release-blocking defects have been closed and retested.
•	No unresolved defect can cause data loss, unauthorized access, incorrect charges, or failure of a critical user journey.
•	Remaining defects are documented in the Go-Live Risk Register.
7. Phase 3: Security Assessment
7.1 Authorization Testing
Test both horizontal and vertical authorization.
Horizontal authorization testing should verify that one user cannot access or modify another user’s:
•	Profile.
•	Orders.
•	Transactions.
•	Files.
•	Messages.
•	Support tickets.
•	Reports.
•	Other user-specific resources.
Vertical authorization testing should verify that ordinary users cannot access:
•	Administrative functions.
•	Support-agent functions.
•	Finance functions.
•	Compliance functions.
•	Internal APIs.
•	Privileged data exports.
•	Configuration or audit functions.
7.2 IDOR Testing
Test all object references used in:
•	URLs.
•	API paths.
•	Request bodies.
•	Query parameters.
•	File identifiers.
•	Download links.
•	Internal record identifiers.
Changing an identifier must not allow unauthorized viewing, modification, deletion, or download of another user’s information.
7.3 Session Management Testing
Test:
•	Session creation and expiration.
•	Logout invalidation.
•	Token rotation.
•	Refresh tokens.
•	Concurrent sessions.
•	Password-change invalidation.
•	Account-deletion invalidation.
•	Session fixation.
•	Token storage on the device.
•	Exposure of tokens in logs, URLs, or analytics.
•	Replay of expired or revoked tokens.
7.4 OTP Testing
Test:
•	OTP expiration.
•	Maximum retry limits.
•	Resend limits.
•	Rate limiting.
•	Brute-force resistance.
•	OTP reuse.
•	OTP interception risks.
•	User enumeration.
•	Verification bypass.
•	Delivery to changed phone numbers or email addresses.
•	Recovery and support-agent override processes.
7.5 Automated Security Testing
Perform and document:
•	Static Application Security Testing, or SAST.
•	Dynamic Application Security Testing, or DAST.
•	Software Composition Analysis for dependencies.
•	Secret scanning.
•	Mobile application security scanning.
•	Container and infrastructure scanning, where applicable.
•	Cloud configuration assessment.
•	API security scanning.
Review findings for false positives and assign each confirmed issue an owner and remediation date.
7.6 Penetration Testing
Conduct risk-based penetration testing of:
•	Android application.
•	APIs.
•	Authentication and account-recovery processes.
•	Administrative interfaces.
•	File upload and download functions.
•	Payment-related functions.
•	Cloud-facing services.
•	Business-logic controls.
•	Abuse and fraud scenarios.
The penetration-test report should contain:
•	Executive summary.
•	Scope and limitations.
•	Methodology.
•	Findings and severity.
•	Evidence.
•	Business impact.
•	Recommended remediation.
•	Retest status.
7.7 Production and Cloud Security Review
Review:
•	Production access permissions.
•	Privileged and administrative accounts.
•	Multi-factor authentication.
•	Service accounts and API keys.
•	Network segmentation.
•	Firewall and security-group rules.
•	Publicly exposed databases or storage.
•	Database authentication and encryption.
•	Encryption in transit and at rest.
•	Secret-management practices.
•	Logging and monitoring.
•	Alerting rules.
•	Cloud audit logs.
•	Backup protection.
•	Separation of production and non-production environments.
•	Exposure of management interfaces.
•	Default accounts and unnecessary services.
7.8 Security Acceptance Standard
The release must have no unresolved Critical or High security vulnerabilities.
An exception may be accepted only when there is a written and formally approved risk-acceptance record containing:
•	The vulnerability and affected assets.
•	The reason immediate remediation is not possible.
•	The assessed business and security impact.
•	Alternative or compensating controls.
•	The accountable risk owner.
•	The person approving the exception.
•	The target remediation date.
•	Monitoring and escalation requirements.
•	The conditions under which the exception must be reconsidered.
8. Phase 4: Compliance and Privacy Audit
8.1 Policy-to-Practice Comparison
Compare actual system behavior with:
•	Privacy policy.
•	Terms and conditions.
•	Data-flow map.
•	Data-retention schedule.
•	Consent records.
•	Google Play Data Safety declaration.
•	Internal security and privacy procedures.
Any difference between declared and actual behavior must be corrected before launch or formally escalated.
8.2 SDK and Endpoint Review
Create an inventory of every:
•	Mobile SDK.
•	Analytics SDK.
•	Advertising SDK.
•	Crash-reporting SDK.
•	Payment SDK.
•	API endpoint.
•	Tracking technology.
•	External service integration.
For each item, document:
•	Provider.
•	Data sent.
•	Purpose.
•	Trigger for transmission.
•	Destination country or region.
•	Whether data leaves Egypt.
•	Whether the provider acts as a third party or processor.
•	Contractual and privacy controls.
•	Retention arrangements.
•	User-consent requirements.
Use network inspection to confirm what data is actually transmitted.
8.3 Account Creation and Deletion Testing
Test:
•	Account creation from the application.
•	Account deletion from the application.
•	Account deletion through the required web link.
•	Identity verification before deletion.
•	Confirmation provided to the user.
•	Removal of active access and sessions.
•	Deletion or anonymization of associated data.
•	Removal from marketing systems.
•	Removal from analytics or downstream systems where required.
•	Treatment of backups.
•	Treatment of legally required retained records.
Only data supported by a documented legal or operational retention requirement should remain after deletion.
For retained data, document:
•	Legal or business justification.
•	Data fields retained.
•	Retention period.
•	Access restrictions.
•	Final deletion process.
8.4 Consent and Withdrawal Testing
Test that:
•	Consent is requested before relevant processing begins.
•	Consent wording is clear and specific.
•	Optional consent is not preselected.
•	Refusal does not block unrelated services.
•	Consent is recorded with date, version, and purpose.
•	Users can withdraw consent.
•	Withdrawal is applied to all relevant systems.
•	Marketing stops after refusal or withdrawal.
•	Previously collected data is handled according to the applicable policy and legal basis.
8.5 Cancellation, Refund, Pricing, and Dark-Pattern Testing
Validate the complete user journey for:
•	Subscription cancellation.
•	Order cancellation.
•	Refund requests.
•	Refund approval or rejection.
•	Refund timing and status.
•	Displayed prices.
•	Taxes, fees, and additional charges.
•	Renewal notices.
•	Free-trial conversion.
•	Confirmation before payment.
Confirm that:
•	Cancellation is not unnecessarily harder than enrollment.
•	Fees are displayed before commitment.
•	Optional purchases are not preselected.
•	Buttons and wording are not misleading.
•	Users are not pressured through false urgency.
•	Important information is not hidden.
•	Consent is not obtained through confusing interfaces.
8.6 User-Generated Content Controls
Where the service contains user-generated content, test:
•	Content reporting.
•	Acknowledgment of reports.
•	Review and moderation.
•	Escalation.
•	Decision recording.
•	Notification of the outcome.
•	User appeal.
•	Appeal review.
•	Account restriction or reinstatement.
•	Retention of moderation evidence.
8.7 Legal Records and Chain of Custody
Test the extraction of records for legal, regulatory, or investigative purposes.
Confirm that:
•	Only authorized personnel can request exports.
•	The export includes only necessary information.
•	Unrelated personal data is excluded.
•	Every action is logged.
•	Exported records are protected.
•	File integrity can be verified.
•	Dates and times are reliable.
•	Custody transfers are documented.
•	Original evidence is preserved.
•	The process does not alter source records.
9. Phase 5: Backup, Recovery, and Cloud Resilience
9.1 Backup and Restore Testing
Test:
•	Database backups.
•	File and object-storage backups.
•	Configuration backups.
•	Encryption-key recovery procedures, where applicable.
•	Restoration to an isolated environment.
•	Full and partial restoration.
•	Recovery from accidental deletion.
•	Recovery from corrupted data.
•	Recovery of dependencies required to operate the service.
Record:
•	Recovery Point Objective.
•	Recovery Time Objective.
•	Actual data loss during the test.
•	Actual recovery duration.
•	Errors and manual steps.
•	Validation that restored data is complete and usable.
A backup should not be treated as successful until restoration has been tested.
9.2 Cloud Provider or Region Failure
Simulate the loss of:
•	A single availability zone.
•	The primary cloud region.
•	A critical managed service.
•	The cloud provider, where a provider-exit or alternative-hosting plan is required.
Validate:
•	Failover procedures.
•	DNS changes.
•	Access to backups from an alternative environment.
•	Recovery of secrets and configuration.
•	Communication procedures.
•	Data integrity.
•	Monitoring and alerting.
•	Manual dependencies and decision authority.
Document any reliance on a single provider, region, account, identity system, or administrator.
10. Phase 6: Security Incident Simulation
Conduct a tabletop or controlled technical simulation involving an incident such as:
•	Unauthorized database access.
•	Leaked API credentials.
•	Compromised administrator account.
•	Exposure of personal data.
•	Malicious application release.
•	Ransomware or destructive deletion.
•	Third-party SDK data leak.
The exercise should test:
1.	Detection.
2.	Initial triage.
3.	Incident classification.
4.	Technical containment.
5.	Credential revocation.
6.	Evidence preservation.
7.	Internal escalation.
8.	Legal and privacy assessment.
9.	User or regulatory notification decision.
10.	Service recovery.
11.	Post-incident review.
Record:
•	Time the incident began.
•	Time of detection.
•	Time of acknowledgment.
•	Time of containment.
•	Time of escalation.
•	Time of recovery.
•	Decisions made.
•	Decision-makers.
•	Evidence used.
•	Communications issued.
•	Gaps identified.
•	Corrective actions and owners.
11. Defect and Finding Management
All defects and findings should be recorded in one controlled register.
Each record should include:
•	Unique ID.
•	Test source.
•	Description.
•	Affected component.
•	Severity.
•	Business impact.
•	Evidence.
•	Reproduction steps.
•	Owner.
•	Target remediation date.
•	Current status.
•	Fix reference.
•	Retest result.
•	Closure approval.
Recommended statuses are:
•	Open.
•	Under Review.
•	Confirmed.
•	In Remediation.
•	Ready for Retest.
•	Closed.
•	Risk Accepted.
•	Duplicate.
•	Not Applicable.
A finding must not be marked closed without documented retest evidence.

The final risk register should include:
Field	Required Information

Source	QA, security, compliance, resilience, or operational testing

Likelihood	Assessed probability
Overall Rating	Agreed risk level
Existing Controls	Controls already in place
Additional Actions	Planned remediation or monitoring
Owner	Accountable individual
Due Date	Target completion date
Acceptance Authority	Person authorized to accept the risk
Review Date	Date on which the risk must be reconsidered
Status	Open, mitigated, accepted, or closed
13. Go-Live Decision Criteria
Go
Production launch may proceed when:
•	Critical user journeys pass.
•	Release-blocking QA defects are closed.
•	No unapproved Critical or High security vulnerabilities remain.
•	Backup restoration has been successfully demonstrated.
•	Production and cloud configuration reviews are complete.
•	Deletion, consent, cancellation, refund, and legal-record processes pass.
•	Required policies and store declarations match actual behavior.
•	Incident-response and recovery exercises are complete.
•	Required reports and evidence have been delivered.
•	Residual risks have named owners and formal approval.
Conditional Go
A conditional launch may proceed only when:
•	The remaining issues do not create an unacceptable risk to users, data, finances, or regulatory compliance.
•	Compensating controls are active.
•	Remediation dates are agreed.
•	Monitoring and rollback conditions are documented.
•	The authorized risk owner signs the exception.
No-Go
Launch must be stopped when:
•	A Critical vulnerability remains unresolved.
•	A High vulnerability remains without approved mitigation and written risk acceptance.
•	Unauthorized data access is possible.
•	Account deletion or consent withdrawal does not work.
•	Pricing, charges, cancellation, or refunds are materially misleading or incorrect.
•	Backups cannot be restored.
•	Production databases or sensitive services are unnecessarily exposed.
•	Actual data processing materially differs from the privacy policy or Data Safety declaration.
•	A critical defect may cause data loss, incorrect charges, or widespread service failure.
•	Required decision-makers have not signed the final approvals.
14. Final Approval
The final go-live approval should be signed by:
•	Product Owner.
•	Engineering Lead.
•	QA Lead.
•	Security Lead.
•	DevOps or Cloud Lead.
•	Privacy or Compliance Lead.
•	Legal representative.
•	Executive Risk Owner.
The approval record should identify:
•	Release version.
•	Production deployment date.
•	Approved residual risks.
•	Conditions attached to approval.
•	Rollback authority.
•	Post-launch monitoring period.
•	Dates for reviewing temporary exceptions.
