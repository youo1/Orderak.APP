MVP Specification and Compliance Planning
Objective
Convert the product idea, operational model, regulatory responsibilities, and app-store requirements into clear product requirements and testable acceptance criteria before development begins.
The development team should not start implementation until the first-version scope, responsibilities, user flows, permissions, and compliance requirements have been reviewed and approved.
________________________________________
Phase 1: Product and Compliance Discovery
1. Define the MVP Scope
Document:
•	The problem the product solves.
•	The target users and markets.
•	The core transaction or user activity.
•	Features included in the MVP.
•	Features explicitly excluded from the MVP.
•	Countries and jurisdictions where the MVP will operate.
•	Whether the product includes payments, user-generated content, minors, regulated services, or sensitive personal data.
2. Identify Users, Roles, and Permissions
Define every user type, such as:
•	Guest user.
•	Registered customer.
•	Merchant or service provider.
•	Customer-support employee.
•	Compliance or risk employee.
•	Operations employee.
•	Administrator.
•	Super administrator.
For every screen and API endpoint, specify:
•	Who can view it.
•	Who can create, edit, approve, cancel, or delete records.
•	Whether additional verification is required.
•	Whether the action must be recorded in an audit log.
•	What happens when access is denied.
Deliverable: Role and Permission Matrix covering both the application interface and APIs.
________________________________________
Phase 2: User Journeys and Core Operations
3. Account and Authentication Journeys
Design and document the complete flows for:
•	Registration.
•	Email or mobile-number verification.
•	Login and logout.
•	OTP generation, delivery, expiration, and retry limits.
•	Password reset and account recovery.
•	Two-factor authentication where required.
•	Suspicious-login detection.
•	Device or session management.
•	Locked, suspended, or compromised accounts.
•	Account deletion.
Include failure cases such as:
•	Incorrect or expired OTP.
•	Duplicate accounts.
•	Unavailable email or phone number.
•	Excessive login attempts.
•	Lost access to the registered email or phone number.
•	Failed identity verification.
4. Core Product Journey
Map the primary end-to-end operation from initiation to completion.
Document:
•	Entry conditions.
•	Required user information.
•	Validation rules.
•	Status changes.
•	Notifications.
•	Completion conditions.
•	Records or evidence created.
•	Cancellation rules.
•	Refund rules.
•	Dispute handling.
•	Failure and retry behaviour.
For each stage, define what happens when:
•	The user leaves the process.
•	A third-party service is unavailable.
•	Payment fails.
•	Verification fails.
•	One party does not complete the required action.
•	The transaction is duplicated.
•	A refund or reversal is requested.
•	A complaint or dispute is opened.
Deliverables: User flows, wireframes, system states, and error-state designs.
________________________________________
Phase 3: Payments, KYC, AML, and Responsibility Allocation
5. Map the Payment Flow
Document the complete movement of money, including:
•	The payer.
•	The payee.
•	The merchant of record.
•	The payment-service provider.
•	Collection, authorization, capture, settlement, and payout.
•	Fees and commissions.
•	Refunds, partial refunds, reversals, and chargebacks.
•	Failed or pending transactions.
•	Reconciliation and financial reporting.
•	Receipts or invoices.
Do not assume that one transaction limit, KYC threshold, or monitoring rule applies to every product or jurisdiction.
6. Assign Regulatory Responsibilities
Agree in writing with the payment provider which party is responsible for:
•	Merchant onboarding.
•	Customer or merchant identity verification.
•	KYC checks.
•	AML screening.
•	Sanctions and watchlist screening.
•	Transaction monitoring.
•	Suspicious-activity investigation and reporting.
•	Transaction limits.
•	Source-of-funds checks where applicable.
•	Record retention.
•	Chargeback management.
•	Fraud monitoring.
•	Regulatory reporting.
•	Responding to law-enforcement or regulator requests.
The agreement must also explain:
•	What information each party collects.
•	What information is exchanged.
•	Which party makes approval or rejection decisions.
•	What happens when a user fails verification.
•	How suspended or restricted funds are handled.
•	How compliance cases are escalated.
Deliverable: Payment, AML, and KYC Responsibility Matrix approved by the product, legal, compliance, finance, and payment-provider teams.
________________________________________
Phase 4: Administration and Operations
7. Define the Administration Dashboard
Specify the tools required by internal employees, including:
•	User search and account review.
•	Account suspension, restriction, and reactivation.
•	Transaction review.
•	Refund and cancellation processing.
•	Dispute and complaint management.
•	Identity-verification review.
•	Content review where applicable.
•	Support-ticket management.
•	Risk alerts.
•	Financial reconciliation.
•	Reporting and exports.
For every sensitive employee action, define:
•	Eligible employee roles.
•	Required reason or case reference.
•	Approval requirements.
•	Whether dual approval is necessary.
•	Data visible before and after the action.
•	Notification requirements.
•	Audit-log requirements.
Sensitive activity logs should capture, where appropriate:
•	Employee identity.
•	Date and time.
•	Affected account or transaction.
•	Previous and new values.
•	Reason for the action.
•	Approval information.
•	Relevant device or session information.

Phase 5: Customer Communications and Rights
8. Specify Customer-Facing Documents and Support
Define requirements for:

•	Transaction confirmations.
•	Email, SMS, push, and in-app notifications.
•	Support channels.
•	Complaint submission.
•	Complaint status tracking.
•	Escalation procedures.
•	Refund notifications.
•	Account restriction notices.
•	Privacy requests.
•	Account deletion and data-retention notices.
Each communication should specify:
•	Triggering event.
•	Recipient.
•	Delivery channel.
•	Required content.
•	Language.
•	Delivery timing.
•	Retry behaviour.
•	Whether delivery evidence must be retained.

Phase 6: User-Generated Content and Minor Safety
9. Add UGC Controls Where Applicable
Where users can publish, upload, message, review, comment, or otherwise provide content, define:
•	Content-reporting mechanisms.
•	Report categories.
•	User blocking.
•	Content removal.
•	Account restriction.
•	Moderator review.
•	Appeal procedures.
•	Repeat-offender handling.
•	Emergency escalation.
•	Record retention.
•	Response-time targets.
Where minors may use or appear in the product, also define:
•	Age-assurance measures.
•	Age-appropriate defaults.
•	Parental or guardian controls where applicable.
•	Contact and messaging restrictions.
•	Advertising restrictions.
•	Location-sharing restrictions.
•	Reporting routes for child-safety concerns.
•	Escalation and preservation procedures.

Phase 7: Google Play and Android Permissions
10. Apply Data and Permission Minimisation
For every Android permission, document:
•	The feature that requires it.
•	The exact point at which it is requested.
•	Whether the feature works without it.
•	The data accessed.
•	How long the data is retained.
•	Whether a lower-risk alternative exists.
•	The user experience after permission denial.
•	The user experience after permanent denial.
Do not request camera, location, microphone, or contact access during installation or initial launch unless access is immediately required for a core feature.
Request access only when the user starts the relevant feature and use the narrowest available permission.
11. Contacts Access
Use Android Contact Picker instead of READ_CONTACTS when users only need to select individual contacts.
For applications targeting Android 17/API 37 or later, prepare for the Google Play requirements scheduled to apply from 28 October 2026 when requesting READ_CONTACTS. The product documentation should demonstrate why Contact Picker is insufficient for an essential feature before broader contact access is approved. [M18]
12. Google Play Billing
Classify every item sold through the application as one of the following:
•	Digital product.
•	Digital service or subscription.
•	Physical product.
•	Physical service.
•	Peer-to-peer or other transaction type requiring separate assessment.
Review Google Play Billing requirements for digital products or services consumed within the application.
Do not use Google Play Billing for the same transaction when the purchase is for qualifying physical goods or physical services. [M19]
Document the billing decision and its justification in the PRD.
13. Target Audience
Define the target audience and age groups before finalizing:
•	User experience.
•	Account creation.
•	Data collection.
•	Advertising.
•	Analytics.
•	Content controls.
•	Communication features.
•	Permissions.
•	Store-listing declarations.
The selected audience must remain consistent across the PRD, application behaviour, privacy disclosures, advertising setup, and Google Play Console declarations.

Phase 8: Data Mapping and Security Requirements
14. Create a Data-to-Feature Matrix
For every feature, document:
•	Data collected.
•	Purpose.
•	Source.
•	Whether collection is mandatory or optional.
•	Legal or contractual basis where applicable.
•	Data recipients.
•	Third-party processors.
•	Storage location.
•	Retention period.
•	Deletion method.
•	Security classification.
•	User controls.
•	Whether the data is declared in the app-store data-safety form.
15. Define Security Requirements
Include requirements for:
•	Encryption in transit and at rest.
•	Password and credential protection.
•	Session expiration and revocation.
•	Rate limiting.
•	Brute-force protection.
•	API authentication and authorization.
•	Protection against duplicate requests.
•	Secure file uploads.
•	Secrets management.
•	Audit logging.
•	Monitoring and alerting.
•	Backup and recovery.
•	Vulnerability management.
•	Incident response.
•	Third-party dependency review.
•	Secure deletion.
•	Separation of production and testing environments.

Phase 9: Acceptance Criteria
16. Write Testable Acceptance Criteria
Every feature must include functional, legal, security, privacy, and operational acceptance criteria.
A feature is not complete merely because the expected interface works.
Each requirement should use a testable format such as:
Given a defined starting condition,
When the user or system performs an action,
Then the expected result occurs,
And the required security, logging, notification, and compliance controls are applied.
Example:
Given that a user has requested an OTP,
When the OTP expires,
Then the system rejects it without authenticating the user,
And records the failed attempt,
And applies the configured retry and rate limits,
And does not reveal whether another account exists.
Acceptance criteria should cover:
•	Successful completion.
•	Invalid input.
•	Permission denial.
•	Service interruption.
•	Duplicate requests.
•	Unauthorized access.
•	Cancellation.
•	Refunds.
•	Data deletion.
•	Audit logging.
•	Notification failure.
•	Regulatory or policy restrictions.

Suggested Delivery Schedule
Week 1: Discovery and Scope
•	Confirm the product model, markets, users, and MVP boundaries.
•	Identify regulated activities, payments, UGC, minors, and sensitive data.
•	Draft user roles and the core journey.
•	Identify unresolved business and compliance decisions.
Week 2: Flows and Responsibilities
•	Complete account, authentication, payment, refund, dispute, and deletion flows.
•	Draft wireframes and error states.
•	Build the Permission Matrix and Data-to-Feature Matrix.
•	Meet the payment provider to allocate KYC, AML, onboarding, monitoring, and reporting responsibilities.
Week 3: Requirements and Acceptance Criteria
•	Complete the PRD.
•	Define administration and operational procedures.
•	Add privacy, security, legal, and Google Play requirements.
•	Write acceptance criteria for every feature and failure scenario.
Week 4: Review and Scope Freeze
•	Conduct product, engineering, security, legal, compliance, operations, and finance reviews.
•	Resolve gaps and conflicting responsibilities.
•	Approve all supporting matrices and diagrams.
•	Freeze the MVP scope and assign change-control ownership.
________________________________________
Approval Gate
Development may begin only after the accountable stakeholders approve:
•	MVP scope.
•	User roles and permissions.
•	Payment and regulatory responsibilities.
•	Data collection and retention.
•	Android permissions.
•	Billing approach.
•	Security requirements.
•	Legal and compliance acceptance criteria.
•	Operational support and escalation procedures.
Any later change affecting payments, permissions, personal data, user eligibility, UGC, minors, or regulated responsibilities must return through legal, compliance, security, and product review before implementation.
